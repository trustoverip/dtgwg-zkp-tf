The third item this issue tracks — coordination with the ZKP task force's blinded-binder work — has a construction record. It is record 008 in the ZKP specification draft, carded from the two routes Scott put on the record in [cred-tf #39](https://github.com/trustoverip/dtgwg-cred-tf/discussions/39) and from @stormer78's E3 enumeration case in [#40](https://github.com/trustoverip/dtgwg-cred-tf/discussions/40). What follows is that record's answer to the three questions above, and the three things the record cannot decide for itself.

**The record, in short.** A verifier that already holds a trust-task context learns that the presented credential was issued inside that exchange; the credential at rest, and every other presentation of it, carry no plaintext binder. Two routes: a salted commitment the verifier opens against the context it supplies, proposed using available primitives, but not yet measured for this record; or a pseudonym derived by a pseudorandom function from the holder's secret and a context descriptor, which is a proposed extension intended to yield the same value within one declared context. Its cross-context privacy remains conditional on the derivation and on what else the presentation exposes. The record states what it does not establish, and the first line matters here: **it blinds the copy the credential carries, not the copy the framework holds.** Enumeration resistance in the credential is not confidentiality of the exchange. A repeated visible commitment can also link presentations without revealing its plaintext. Route 1 must therefore specify whether the commitment remains public, is hidden in the proof, or is rerandomized with a verifiable relation; unlinkability is not established by salting alone.

**On the first tracked question — whether WD03 adds a salt, and where it lives.** From the proof side the requirement is not "a salt" but three properties, and only the third is a real constraint on this specification:

1. *Enough entropy.* The quantity that matters is the min-entropy of the hashed preimage, not the strength of the hash. For resistance to offline guessing by a verifier without the opening, the proposal assumes an independent, uniformly random 128-bit secret blinding value. A public salt does not stop that verifier guessing a low-entropy context. The record must specify which parties receive or retain the opening; deriving it from public credential fields does not supply secret entropy.
2. *Inside the hashed block.* The blinding value has to be part of what the digest covers, so the digest binds it. Its placement in the preimage does not imply that the opening should be disclosed with every presentation. A self-addressing identifier alone does not help — it changes how the digest is named, not how much entropy it covers. ACDC's `u` is exactly the right shape.
3. *One encoding, agreed once.* The record's issuance clause asks that the commitment be `digestMultibase`-encoded per D-A in #31, so the two layers agree on the bytes before either of them argues about the construction. This is the reason #19 landing the encoding without changing what is hashed was the right order: a blinding scheme added at the canonicalization step later costs no second migration.

One further clause the record would like in WD03, because it is cheap and it closes the obvious failure: **a credential that carries a blinded binder must not also carry its plaintext.** The record calls for the lint refusal `binder-plaintext-present`; a record-specific implementation and fixture must still demonstrate that check.

**On the second — whether one construction serves anchoring, revocation status and registry membership too.** Partly, and the part that generalises is the encoding rather than the construction. A blinded binder is a *commitment opened against a value the verifier supplies*. Registry membership and revocation status are *set statements*: a signed, published root plus a membership or non-membership witness carried in the presentation, with no live lookup, which is record 001 and record 006 in the draft. They are the same primitive family — commit, then prove something about what was committed — and they are different records because their adversaries and their horizons differ: a binder's horizon is the exchange's thread, a status root's horizon is its publication epoch. Unifying them at the level of "how the digest is written" is right; unifying them at the level of "one construction" would hide that difference.

**On the third — coordination.** Record 008 is the artifact. Its costs are labelled as conjecture, not measurement, and that is the honest state: the salted-commitment route is one hash opening in-circuit, which the lab's Poseidon gadget makes cheap, but the binder as WD02 hashes it is JCS-SHA-256, and a standard hash opened inside a circuit is the expensive case this task force has flagged as X3. There are two ways out and the specification should not pick one yet: commit in a proof-friendly hash for the committed form only, or use a proving system that proves standard hashes cheaply — the draft carries Flock as such an entry, with author-reported benchmarks for batched standard-hash computation. Those figures do not measure this credential construction. Neither is measured by us. When one is, it will be a registry row rather than a claim.

Happy to take any of this as a pull request against WD03 once the task force settles whether the salt member is normative for all three binder types or only where a verifier is shown a digest without the referenced credential.
