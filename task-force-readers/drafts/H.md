The working group's specification repository for this task force — `trustoverip/dtgwg-zkp-spec`, set up on 2 September on the ToIP Spec-Up-T template — is the natural home for the deliverable. What follows is a proposal for its shape. A pull request is drafted and will open once the shape has been argued with.

**The unit is a construction record.** One per zero-knowledge proof the trust graph needs. Each states what a verifier learns, from whom, without what; the witness that never leaves the holder; the public inputs; numbered method clauses, each bound to a named gadget; the disclosure set; what the proof does not establish; the adversary each privacy claim is made against and the horizon that bounds it; the conformance fixture families; the construction options across proving systems, with cost measured or conjectured; what the construction requires of issuers; provenance. The task force's four drafting rules are fields of the record. A validator refuses a record that lacks any of them, and a record that does not validate does not render.

**A record's state says how much weight it bears.** `requested → carded → constructed → run → vetted → published`. Everything in the first draft is at `carded` or `constructed` and is informative. Normative language enters at `vetted` — a verification-registry row from a party other than the constructor — and RECOMMENDED for a proving system only when independent parties have reproduced a construction on it across architectures.

**The chapters.** Requests Answered — ADR-001 first, in its author's own form, with a clause-by-clause crosswalk into the construction that answers it (17 clauses covered, 5 refined, 2 added by the task force: S6 the voucher is not the holder, S7 common control across a party's identifiers; 3 partial; 4 open to the registry and governance groups) · Cryptographic Background · Public Inputs — shared conventions (context descriptor, set roots, epoch, transcript digest under RFC 8785 and digestMultibase, declared correlation scope, a public-signal order offered for ratification) · Construction Records — twelve: eight primitive, four composed · Proving Systems — the reference Groth16 lab, ProveKit, the SIROS catalog, Flock: facts with sources and a verification date, never recommendations · Security, Privacy (in part generated from the records' adversary fields), Governance, Internationalization and Accessibility Considerations · Conformance · References.

**The validation system travels with the text.** The repository gains a `conformance/` directory: the records, requests and proving-system entries as JSON, the schema, a zero-dependency validator whose refusals are register strings, and a continuous-integration check that fails when the generated sections of the specification no longer match the records they were generated from — a digest of the records is stamped in the text. Changes go to records, never to generated prose. Conformance targets and tests are written as normative text: the record, the constructor, the prover, the verifier, the issuer and registry, the proving-system entry; the validator, the freshness check, the fixture format (a versioned rejection-reason register, accept/reject/lint vectors, a consumer in a second language with no shared code), independent reproduction through the registry's acceptance flow, and the reproduction ladder.

**The split.** Specification content lives in `dtgwg-zkp-spec`. Requirements, drafting rules, the board and these discussions stay in this repository. Runtimes, fixtures, the verification registry and the generator stay in the evidence repository, which the specification cites and never depends on. The relationship between the three, and the evidence repository's book edition of the same records, is laid out on #14.

Objections to the shape are the useful reply: chapter order, the record form, whether the conformance apparatus belongs inside the specification repository or beside it, and whether the state ladder is the right gate for normative language. Content objections per construction can follow as issues once the pull request is open. The form is cheap to change at twelve records and expensive at fifty.
